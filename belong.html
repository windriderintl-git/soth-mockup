Our welcome

All are welcome. No exceptions.

A lot of churches say "all are welcome" and mean "most." We name people on purpose, because too many of us have been told, out loud or quietly, that church wasn't for us. It is.

Our welcome statement

We echo Jesus' welcome to all persons, everyone.

All are welcome at Shepherd of the Hills. We are a place where all people can receive wholeness and share God's blessings:

To the devout, the seeker, and the skeptic To single, married, widowed, and divorced To young and old, child and parent To persons of all sexual orientations and gender identities To people of all lands, cultures, and colors To people with disabilities, those ill and well To poor, middling, and wealthy To all who seek hope, peace, comfort, strength, love, forgiveness, and grace

We invite everyone to worship, learn, serve, and care with us.

Welcome is a practice

What that welcome looks like on an actual Sunday.

A statement is just words until it changes how a community behaves. Here's how ours does.

LGBTQ+ people are affirmed, not debated

Full participation in worship, communion, baptism, leadership, marriage, and service. Your identity is not an "issue" here. You're a neighbor.

Doubt is honored

Seekers and skeptics are named in our welcome on purpose. Questions are how faith breathes, and nobody will hand you a script.

Access is hospitality

Accessible parking and entry, communion brought to your seat, worship on Zoom, and a congregation that adjusts to people, not the reverse.

Kids are full participants

The PrayGround, children's messages, and Coffee Hour Buddies treat children as the church of today, not just the church of someday.

No wallet test

"Poor, middling, and wealthy" is in our welcome statement. Nothing at SOTH has a price of admission: not communion, not coffee, not community.

Neighbors beyond our walls

More than twenty community partnerships (food shelves, housing, mental health) because welcome that stops at the door isn't welcome.

What we believe

Four sentences we mean.

Love, not doctrine, is at the center of it all.

Doctrine matters, but it serves love, never the other way around. When in doubt, we err on the side of grace.

We take the Bible seriously, but we don't take it literally.

Scripture is a living conversation between God and God's people. We study it, wrestle with it, and let it read us back.

We are deeply rooted in the Lutheran tradition, and we appreciate the wisdom of other traditions.

Grace first, always. And the humility to learn from every tradition that seeks God honestly.

Caring for our neighbors is central to following Jesus.

Faith that doesn't show up for the neighborhood isn't finished. Service isn't a program here. It's the point.

Our church family

Part of something bigger.

Shepherd of the Hills is a congregation of the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA) and the Minneapolis Area Synod, a church body committed to grace, justice, and the dignity of every person.
